# EARTH-43 — recovery workstation

The user requested a clearer Earth-side scene: a computer, as if at NASA,
showing Opportunity overhead in the storm. Two GPT-6.1 Sol implementation lanes
built a physical workstation shell and integrated the existing rover renderer.
A separate Sol critic reviewed desktop, short-screen and portrait captures.

The monitor frames the same rover and terrain, with an overhead view and storm
lighting. Its brief “Last known position / Reconstruction” label avoids implying
live contact after the rover went silent. The dark desk, bezel, stand and keyboard
make the location change visible without adding explanatory panels. The original
Earth-to-Mars call, no-reply result and archive controls remain on the desk below.

The first composition failed the clarity goal: Opportunity was too small and dark,
and a brighter outcrop competed with it. The revised framing enlarges the rover
about 1.5 times and adds restrained local fill while retaining storm dust and fog.
The critic accepted the resulting subject clarity and physical workstation on
phone and desktop. Short-screen planet clipping was corrected; the lower console
retains scrolling for receipts and controller prompts. Its scrollbar was muted
following review.

This is an authored mission-control setting, not a recreation of a particular
NASA room or instrument display. Physical controller feel and the emotional
pacing of a fresh full playthrough still need human playtesting.
