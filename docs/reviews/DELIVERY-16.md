# DELIVERY-16 — let the completed action speak

Source review only: required project documents; main, terminal-story, time, briefing, story-console, sound, experience and session modules. No playthrough, controller test or listening test. Proposals below are script changes, not implemented features.

## Diagnosis

The problem is phase churn as much as word count. The twelve-second introduction repeats identity, water and sending in first-morning’s opening thought. Its three beats last four seconds each. The eleven-year passage compresses three similarly substantial beats into seven seconds: 2.33 seconds each, while reveal can consume roughly two seconds. The player barely finishes seeing a line before replacement.

During manual travel, `travelling` reaches `storyLine` but does not change its output: the geological question persists before arrival and alongside navigation. Active investigations correctly suppress thought. Completion then auto-selects the recorded discovery: interpretation occupies trigger preparation, a generic sending thought replaces it during the 2.4-second transmission, and receipt restates it. Dust similarly explains sunlight loss before action, after action and after receipt. Ordinary-day already mourns light before the rupture. This repeated explanation makes each action feel like advancing captions.

The writer preserves reveal across rerenders, but a new phase replaces unfinished prose. Completed thoughts remain until another phase. Neither behavior supplies an authored silent interval by itself.

## Event script

1. **Opening:** first intro beat: “I am Opportunity.” Second: “I came to look for ancient water.” Third: “Earth is waiting for my first view.” Keep existing camera shots and Skip. On first playable observe, replace the identity recap with “What shall I show them first?” The existing camera action and view choices explain what to do even after Skip. No text promises a live conversation.
2. **Travel / arrival:** keep navigation imperative outside thought. Silence while driving to Last Chance and Berry Bowl. On arrival, use the existing `reached`/`travelling` distinction to supply one question: crossing layers; grains embedded in rock. This requires honoring an existing argument, not inventing an arrival callback. Never stack a second arrival caption.
3. **Hands-on geology:** leave thoughts blank during tracing, brushing, sampling and comparison. Tool cues describe movement and progress. At completed comparison, trigger-ready thought names the observed difference only: “The grains read differently from the brushed rock.” Last Chance names the crossing layers. No compulsory reading delay. At successful receipt give the single qualified interpretation: layers are evidence of ancient flowing water; hematite grains are another clue. Let Continue hold that essential inference. Detailed explanation stays optional.
4. **Send / return:** keep the observed record visible while charging. Trigger release clears thought; packets and the learned question occupy departure. Remove the universal “A little of my world…” line. First return earns one relationship thought: “They have my first view.” Later routine receipts use operational acknowledgement without another companionship paragraph. The ordinary afternoon stays ordinary. Preserve the 2.4-second transaction and existing 2.2-second audio answer window; no new narrative timer.
5. **Years / storm:** one thought spans the seven-second eleven-year passage; remaining light cycles are silent. Three-year passage supplies the new age once. Dust’s pre-action thought names blocked sunlight and panel dependence once; survey and receipt add no explanation. Smaller-world recalls the actual first selection once.
6. **Contact loss:** final ready thought is brief; departure and partial receipt have no thought. Preserve partial-image status. The existing handover explicitly places the player on Earth; its destination subtitle must retain role/action after Skip or reload. Recovery calls keep route, waiting and NO REPLY; no imagined rover voice fills the missing answer.
