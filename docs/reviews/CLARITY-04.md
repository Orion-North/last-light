## Three highest-value remaining changes

1. **Name the measurement in Berry Bowl's existing discovery sentence (scientific clarity / design recommendation).** `src/content/story.js` currently reveals “Round grains contain hematite” after the player visually orbits the grains. The optional journal correctly names spectrometers, but the mandatory loop never distinguishes seeing grains from measuring their composition. Replace the existing sentence, without adding another screen: “Spectrometers found hematite in the round grains; comparison with brushed rock supported formation involving water.” This stays within SOURCES.md N7 and avoids teaching that visible shape or color identifies hematite. Keep the goal “Find the round grains beside the clear patch.” Observation and inference then have distinct roles without prose bloat.

2. **Derive contact objectives from receipt mode (concrete text inconsistency).** `src/ui/objectives.js:7` uses `scene.signal` while `transmissionProfile` uses `receiptMode`. During last-image, a partial-image transmission therefore asks the player to “Listen for Earth’s reply.” That scene deliberately supplies no imagined reply. Branch on the same mode used by the receipt: partial can say “Wait for the image to arrive”; reply can keep the existing instruction; silence retains the Earth call. This reinforces the learned routine exactly where its failure matters. Check the objective, waiting caption and receipt together in a muted playthrough.

3. **Keep environment reflections consistent as the sky darkens (asset/rendering defect).** `src/ui/landscape.js:384` captures a PMREM environment once from the initial sky. `applyPalette` later changes sky uniforms, exposure and lights, but never refreshes the environment or its intensity. Metallic rover hardware therefore continues reflecting the initial bright atmosphere through storm/night scenes. Exposure can dim it but cannot give it the new sky coloration. Prefer a small set of precomputed environment states, or explicitly fade `environmentIntensity` with the authored palette; avoid regenerating PMREM every frame. This is a source-level rendering mismatch; whether it is visually distracting remains for root's browser QA. Do not add sky textures or downloads to solve it.

## Engagement and usability judgments

The two geological interactions now ask for an active change of view, followed by a forgiving steady hold. This is an appropriate small action for the scope. It is still camera-position recognition, not scientific measurement or a tool simulation; describe the objective accordingly. Adding an antenna puzzle, a timing challenge, extra mandatory scenes or a survival cost would obscure the user's request. The next useful evidence is whether a new player can find the layers/grains and explain why they matter, not an extra paragraph.

Inspection sampling scores camera position against surface normal and distance, plus steadiness (`landscape.js:752–765`); it does not separately verify screen-space visibility or occlusion. Normal flow fixes the orbit target, so this is a robustness limitation rather than a demonstrated remaining player failure. If camera controls expand, validate target projection/view direction before awarding discovery. The changing camera-reset fix should be included in the controller journey check.

All six acquired maps matched the official MD5/size and 1024×1024 JPEG dimensions in ART-04. Albedo uses sRGB; normals/roughness use linear data; terrain's 80 repeats across 240m matches the source's 3m width. Shared rock maps bound GPU cost. Rock silhouettes remain authored geometry, not scanned meshes. The shader sky's dust bands/veils and blue halo are artistic approximations; exact atmospheric physics is outside the stated contract. Root should judge close-up texture contrast against legibility of authored geological relief, and check the phone camera fit, storm and final darkness. This audit makes no claim that those visual checks passed.

## Actual-render review — supplied v0.4 still

Viewed `/home/_0r1/Documents/Codex/2026-11-14/look/outputs/last-light-v04-draft.jpg` directly. This is one 1280×720 Last Chance memory-choice frame, not an interactive playtest. Two concrete visual fixes take priority:

1. **Break the terrain's repeated high-frequency pattern at distance.** The far slope reads as evenly repeated horizontal rows of the same coarse gravel, and the foreground carries almost the same sharp dark mottling. It makes the environment look tiled and overscaled despite the higher-resolution source. Add a second low-frequency material/color mask with a different scale/rotation, vary rock/gravel coverage, and fade fine normal/color contrast with distance. Keep the 3m physical source scale for nearby detail; do not simply stretch the same map over the whole landscape. The hills should resolve into broad dusty landforms while the immediate ground retains a few readable stones.

2. **Give the investigation outcrop a quieter, distinct sedimentary surface.** The central target and background boulders currently share strongly saturated red-orange, cracked, heavy surface detail. Its dark branching fissures compete with the thin crossing laminae the player is meant to notice. Use a lighter, less saturated tan-gray material for Last Chance, reduce its generic rock normal strength and albedo contrast independently of background rocks, and let the authored crossing layer relief provide the dominant directional structure. In this still the target's silhouette is readable, but identifying the specific water-evidence feature is harder than reading the generic cracking. This is an information-design issue as well as material polish.

The sky occupies only a narrow gray strip in this supplied view, so this image cannot establish whether the sky is realistic or interesting. No additional sky verdict is justified from this frame. The image still reads as a stylized game environment with conspicuous texture repetition; the scanned maps alone have not established a consistently high-quality natural landscape.
