# UX-03 — attention and information delivery

Baseline: `3aeb99c`. Reviewed instructions, plans, runtime, input, presentation, and supplied v0.2 screenshot. This is source/screenshot inspection, not a playtest, contrast measurement, or controller/phone hardware test. Recommendations and predicted player problems below are design inferences.

Reference evidence: Campo Santo describes the handheld radio as Firewatch's only human connection ([official FAQ](https://www.firewatchgame.com/about/)). Its developers' [GDC session abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022108/Level-Design-in-a-Day) identifies exploration, expressive choices, goals, gating, and encounters as deliberate design concerns; I did not watch the talk. Starward's [official store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/731040/The_Invincible/) couples discoveries with reporting to an Astrogator and interacting with analogue telemeters/trackers. These support communication and instruments as playable activities, not claims about either game's exact HUD/accessibility behavior.

## Prioritized findings

1. **P1 — Make the mission/mechanics boundary visible during play.** `src/main.js:91–92` presents precise LIGHT/LINK percentages, while the explicit dramatization label is inside the optional journal (`:134`). The engine says progression never depends on timers or power failure (`src/engine/game.js:2`); zero signal disables driving (`src/main.js:271`), but exploring does not consume these scene-authored percentages. This can suggest a resource-management responsibility the player does not have. Borrow The Invincible's instrument-purpose relationship: label the display “Dramatized mission conditions” and introduce one sentence: “Explore at your pace; this mission's ending is fixed.” Keep detailed history optional. **Success:** a first-time player can explain whether driving wastes power and whether they can save Opportunity without opening the journal.

2. **P1 — Give the scan an observable subject and result.** `src/main.js:149–161` runs a one-second scan before replacing the dock with discovery/choices; the reticle is screen-centered (`src/ui/play.css:48`), independent of the observation's visible location. Arrival itself becomes a prompt and cue (`src/main.js:303–306`). The Invincible's official analogue-tool framing suggests an affordable improvement: frame the actual nearby outcrop, briefly identify the measurement, then pair a visible feature with one scientific inference. Preserve instant reduced-motion progression. **Success:** after scanning, players can point to what was examined and name the evidence supporting the discovery.

3. **P2 — Teach one action at a time in the player's current input language.** Keyboard help is tiny (`index.html:41`), disappears below 850px (`src/ui/play.css:56`), and controller help always uses PlayStation glyphs even for generic standard pads (`src/main.js:348–349`; `src/input/controller.js:2`). Introduce brief contextual “Drive to beacon,” “Scan,” then “Choose what to send” prompts with detected-device labels; retain full controls in Settings. Firewatch's radio-centered premise suggests concentrating guidance around the current meaningful action. **Success:** new touch, keyboard, and controller users complete the first loop without consulting Settings or guessing a glyph.

4. **P2 — Consolidate attention around the current task.** The screenshot splits title, waypoint, telemetry, interaction, and controls across five regions; `src/main.js:123` also exposes “3 / 12” as persistent progress bookkeeping. Keep the landscape and strong existing dock; combine destination/distance/action there, subordinate scene count, and surface telemetry changes when relevant. Apply Firewatch's explicit exploration/encounter framing rather than importing its layout. **Success:** players identify their next action within three seconds while still noticing the observation in the world.

5. **P2 — Let interface scale preserve guidance.** Mobile rules shrink telemetry to 8px and targets to 36×38px (`src/ui/play.css:65–73`); multiple HUD regions remain. Add a readable interface-size option, larger touch controls, and a compact mode that removes secondary information before shrinking text. Preserve existing focus outlines (`:15`), sound-off text feedback, and reduced motion. This is an accessibility inference, not a verified feature of the references. **Success:** first-loop tasks remain readable and operable at 200% zoom and on actual small-screen touch hardware without obscuring the scan target.
