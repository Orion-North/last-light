# EMOTION-03 — attachment through action

Review of `3aeb99c`: required project documents, source, and supplied v0.2 screenshot only. No playthrough, reference-game play claim, or audio audition. Findings below are design interpretations, not measured player responses.

## Reference facts

[Journey’s developer page](https://thatgamecompany.com/journey/) describes an anonymous passage, possible companions, a visible mountain destination, and movement through desert and ruins. [Jenova Chen’s GDC session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1018751/Designing) explicitly concerns iteration toward an emotional arc; the listing alone does not substantiate detailed mechanics. [FAR: Lone Sails’ official page](https://far-game.com/) describes keeping a vehicle going through roadblocks and hazardous weather, following a lost civilization’s tracks. My interpretation: these references make passage, dependence, and environment carry emotional information. They do not establish that Last Light needs multiplayer, survival management, or their production scale.

## Prioritized findings

1. **P1 — Make the familiar routine change before naming the loss.** Evidence: `src/main.js:149` makes a scan a one-second timer; `src/ui/landscape.js:499` frames the rover rather than the discovery. Driving speed remains constant at `landscape.js:550` until zero power/signal disables it (`455`). Storm power is declared in `src/content/story.js:140`. FAR’s source-backed technique is maintaining movement against changing conditions. Interpretation: Last Light currently changes atmosphere more than the player's practiced relationship with the machine. Reuse one early camera/aim/send action during the storm, with a visibly incomplete exposure or interrupted return; retain assisted and reduced-motion equivalents and no failure penalty. **Test:** without reading telemetry, players can identify which previously reliable action changed and explain why.

2. **P1 — Let Earth become a learned response, then an absence.** Evidence: Earth replies are prose (`story.js:26`, `136`); the received phase renders the same decorative waveform for any nonzero signal (`main.js:121`). Journey’s official emphasis on companionship suggests a useful principle: attachment needs a perceptible other. Establish a short, consistent received-signal light/pulse paired with the fictional reply early; let the last transmission visibly send without receiving that pulse. Existing explicit silence states (`src/audio/sound.js:87`) provide a foundation, but their effect is unverified. **Test:** muted players notice the missing response before a caption announces silence; sound-on players associate the returning cue with Earth rather than generic success.

3. **P1 — Turn memory choices into things the player actually made.** Evidence: horizon versus ground creates authored text (`story.js:38`); selection is rendered as buttons (`main.js:117`), and the ending counts memories (`145`). Journey’s source-backed traversal and FAR’s physical vessel suggest agency grounded in action. Interpretation: choosing a sentiment may produce less attachment than recognizing one's own work. Let players frame one view or choose a visible detail; preserve that crop/scene state as a small archive image and recall it during the storm. **Test:** after the ending, players recognize their own selected detail and describe why they chose it without consulting its written title.

4. **P2 — Give the ending room to be inhabited.** Evidence: zero-signal scenes retain observe/choose/listen/next gates (`main.js:112`); “Stay a little longer” still advances a scene (`121`). Several concluding captions explain the meaning (`story.js:227`, `249`, `271`). Journey’s stated destination and Chen’s emotional-arc framing support deliberate pacing, not additional exposition. After one final attempt, offer an unhurried view of tracks and the chosen image, then one clear voluntary remembrance action. Keep factual epilogue optional and accessible. **Test:** players describe an action or absence as the emotional turning point; they can finish without opening the journal and do not report repeated closing beats.
